# 0. PURPOSE

This document resolves the major content-system issues identified in the site-wide copy audit and establishes the working product/editorial canon for the next Red Clay copy rewrite.

It exists to prevent:

- repeated generic brand language;
- interchangeable coffee products;
- internal production/provenance language leaking into customer copy;
- inconsistent terminology;
- undefined commerce states;
- underdeveloped Editions;
- copy written around temporary assets instead of around products, places, and reader needs.

This document is the primary working authority for product and editorial copy during the next rewrite pass.

Where this document conflicts with older scaffolds, this document takes precedence for the subjects it explicitly resolves.

This document does not override the master visual art direction, The Earthen Folio interaction specification, approved asset provenance rules, accessibility requirements, or the prohibition against implying real sourcing relationships.

# 1. CORE BRAND THESIS

## Formal thesis

Red Clay is a contemporary African coffee house that expresses the architectural materiality of place through exceptional coffees, tactile objects, and publication-grade storytelling.

## Short public thesis

**Coffee, held by place.**

## Brand continuum

**EARTH → COFFEE → VESSEL → RITUAL**

The full explanation belongs primarily on `/about`. Elsewhere, use only restrained echoes when useful.

## Emotional territory

- grounded calm;
- architectural substance;
- luminous warmth;
- tactile usefulness;
- thoughtful hospitality.

## Voice

Red Clay should sound assured, tactile, literate, grounded, warm, precise, useful, and observant.

Red Clay should feel like:

> a knowledgeable host helping the visitor notice, choose, understand, and continue.

Red Clay should not sound slang-heavy, mystical, paternalistic, self-important, like internal art-direction documentation, or like generic luxury-brand copy.

---

# 2. COPY PRINCIPLE

Every customer-facing section should primarily do at least one of four jobs:

## INFORMATION
Teach something useful.

## DIFFERENTIATION
Explain why this coffee, place, object, or story is distinct.

## ORIENTATION
Help the visitor understand where they are and where they can go next.

## ATMOSPHERE
Create a deliberate emotional pause.

Atmosphere is valid, but it must be used selectively.

## Central editorial test

> **Why does this sentence need to exist here, for this visitor, at this exact point in their journey?**

If there is no strong answer, remove it, consolidate it, make it specific, or give it a clearer customer job.

Red Clay is a fictional portfolio brand.

Do not repeat this fact throughout product pages, dossiers, articles, or object descriptions.

Use one restrained site-level disclosure.

## Approved working disclosure

> **Red Clay Coffee is an independently created fictional brand concept. Regional editorial content is informed by researched coffee context. People and locations shown in documentary imagery do not represent commercial Red Clay sourcing relationships.**

Recommended placement:

- footer legal/project note;
- dedicated portfolio disclosure;
- or equivalent globally accessible location.

Do not insert repeated copy such as “fictional Red Clay product expression,” “approved plate,” “current visual record,” “replaceable media,” “provenance kept close to the image,” or “this image does not imply a Red Clay sourcing relationship” inside ordinary customer-facing prose.

# 4. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

Red Clay should no longer behave like a four-coffee demo shop.

The product universe has four families:

1. **THE MATERIAL SERIES**
2. **CURRENT HARVEST**
3. **OTHER WAYS TO DRINK**
4. **OBJECTS & EDITIONS**

The catalog should feel curated rather than large. Do not render all products as one uniform grid.

# 5. THE MATERIAL SERIES

## Role

A permanent house collection that gives customers recognizable coffees they can return to.

The underlying East African components may rotate with season while the intended cup profile remains stable.

These are profile-led house coffees, not origin-fiction products.

Material names are brand metaphors. They must never imply that the named material physically causes flavor.

## 5.1 LATERITE

**Status:** APPROVED WORKING PRODUCT
**Family:** The Material Series
**Role:** House Coffee
**Availability model:** Ongoing / rotating seasonal composition
**Primary use:** Filter, batch, espresso
**Formats:** 250g / 1kg
**Working profile:** Red plum / brown sugar / cacao

**Product character:** Warm, balanced, generous, and easy to return to.

**Working public description:** Laterite is Red Clay's centre of gravity: generous sweetness, vivid fruit, and enough depth to remain an everyday coffee.

**Customer role:** Default recommendation for balance, sweetness, versatility, and a first Red Clay coffee.

**Guardrail:** Do not imply laterite soil creates this flavor profile.

## 5.2 BASALT

**Status:** APPROVED WORKING PRODUCT
**Family:** The Material Series
**Role:** House Espresso

# 6. CURRENT HARVEST

## Role

Transparent seasonal releases.

These coffees should use **region + process + release number** rather than fictional farm or washing-station names.

This protects Red Clay from inventing real producer relationships while still allowing the product catalog to feel credible and specific.

## Naming pattern

`REGION / PROCESS ##`

Examples:

- `KIAMBU / WASHED 01`
- `GUJI / NATURAL 02`

## Current Harvest collection term

Use **Current Harvest** for the active seasonal collection.

Use **coffee** in ordinary customer-facing prose.

Use **lot** only when factually appropriate.

Use **release** sparingly for an active product.

Avoid using `seasonal`, `current lot`, `current release`, and `current coffee` interchangeably without purpose.

# 11. PRODUCT DECISION COPY

Coffee product pages should follow this content hierarchy:

1. **IDENTITY** — What is it?
2. **CHARACTER** — What does it broadly taste/feel like?
3. **TASTING NOTES** — What sensory references help distinguish it?
4. **PURCHASE FACTS** — What does the customer need to act?
5. **PLACE / PROCESS** — What context helps deepen understanding?
6. **RELATED STORY** — Where can the visitor learn more?
7. **RELATED COFFEE** — What should they compare it with?

Do not place long abstract brand philosophy ahead of basic product clarity.

- higher elevation causes floral flavor;
- natural process creates strawberry notes;
- washed process creates citrus;
- one variety guarantees one sensory outcome.

Use language such as:

- can contribute;
- is often associated with;
- provides one variable;
- is part of the context;
- may influence;
- helps explain.

Where scientific nuance is not useful to the customer, simply avoid the causal claim.
